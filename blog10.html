Blog #10: LLMs Are a Race to the Bottom. So Who Wins?

28 August 2026

First publushed on Medium here.

If you're in the AI/ML social media space, you've already seen the Google Gemini memes. And they are funny.

Indeed, if we look at agent performance on Arena, Google's highest-ranked model is in 27th place as of August 28, 2026. But something else stands out on the leaderboard: open models like DeepSeek V4, Kimi K3, Qwen3.8, and GLM 5.2 are competitive with closed models from Anthropic and OpenAI.

Further, the open models are pennies on the dollar per-token compared to closed models, and they were almost certainly pennies on the dollar to train. I'm no economist, but the writing is on the wall: frontier-lab inference is becoming incredibly cheap.

Open-model tokens are filling up the moat; consumers will soon be strolling into the LLM castle.

Google's Strategic Dilemma

Now let's re-examine Google's position. The race is likely lost, but it's a race to the bottom. Why not quit while you're behind and supply the running shoes? Google provides premium infrastructure in Google Cloud and (sub?)premium hardware in TPUs.

Ironically, LLMs are an existential threat to Google. Chat interfaces are rapidly replacing Google Search. Search advertisements made up 56% of Google's $400B FY25 revenue. Google Cloud made up a measly 14%, though I expect this ratio to decrease year-over-year.

Long story short, Google needs to carefully consider its strategy around LLMs, but building a better one is probably not the solution. And it's possible infrastructure and hardware won't be the saving grace either.

The Inevitable Move to Local Inference

Three simultaneous trends will increasingly drive inference to local machines:

  1. Exceptional open models: As discussed, open models are becoming exceptional.
  2. Radical efficiency: Those exceptional open models have much smaller footprints during inference, and clever engineering tricks can shrink them further without major performance drawbacks (quantization, KV cache optimization).
  3. Commoditized specialized hardware: AI-specialized computers are rapidly commoditizing, offering massive unified memory with acceptable FLOPs (see Mac Studio, Framework Desktop).

Foolish Apple that sat back and waited while big tech sprinted ahead with LLMs may be among the biggest AI winners.

Who Survives the Race?

Does this mean hyperscalers like Microsoft are in trouble? Not necessarily. Inference may become increasingly local, but pre-training will rely on massive data centers for the foreseeable future. And unlike Google, Microsoft's historic revenue streams like Windows and Office are empowered by LLMs.

So who wins an LLM race to the bottom? The companies providing the infrastructure for the race and the companies applying the cheap products of the race. It's no coincidence that OpenAI is going local and Anthropic is going bio. They know an LLM-only company cannot last much longer.

A Dangerous Thought: The Regulatory Moat

I'll leave you with a dangerous thought. We see OpenAI and Anthropic becoming increasingly vocal on AI safety, around the same time open Chinese models are going toe-to-toe with the closed American models…

With a closed model, one could presumably provide a neutered version with a government-approved stamp. For open models, the risk of guardrail removal could be used to ban them outright.

The only way an LLM-only company can win is with an unfair advantage.
