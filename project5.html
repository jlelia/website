Project #5: MechMap - Generative Representation Learning for Drug Mechanism of Action Discovery

Built With: Python 3 | PyTorch | scikit-learn | conditional variational autoencoders (CVAEs) | supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) | LLM API integration

Practical Objective: Integrate disparate cancer pharmacologic screens (PRISM, CTD², GDSC1, GDSC2) into a unified, mechanism-aware latent manifold, enabling scalable mechanism-of-action (MoA) discovery.

Learning Objective: Develop expertise in generative representation learning and metric learning on high-dimensional biomedical data while building rigorous, leakage-free benchmarks.

Purpose of MechMap

High-throughput phenotypic drug discovery routinely yields highly potent small molecules whose biological targets remain unknown or poorly characterized. Wet-lab target deconvolution is low-throughput, expensive, and difficult to scale across thousands of compounds.

Large-scale pharmacogenomic consortia (e.g. the Broad and Sanger Institutes) screened thousands of small molecules across hundreds of genetically characterized cancer cell lines. However, cross-screen data integration faces two critical bottlenecks:

  • Severe Assay and Platform Batch Effects: Response metrics (AUC, IC50) are dominated by differences in screening protocol, readout technology, incubation time, and culture media. When analyzed directly, compounds cluster primarily by screening platform rather than biological mechanism.
  • Minimal Compound and Cell Line Overlap: Across four major screening platforms (PRISM, CTD², GDSC1, GDSC2), only 43/2021 compounds and 287/1232 cell lines are tested in common across all four. Further, screen composition is heavily biased, so removing batch also removes biology.

I developed MechMap in the Bindra Laboratory at Yale to overcome these limitations. By converting pharmacological sensitivity into functional genetic dependency fingerprints and embedding them into a latent manifold, MechMap enables mechanism-level queries and target deconvolution across independent screening platforms.

Functional Fingerprinting via DepMap CRISPR Dependencies

MechMap constructs biological association vectors using genome-scale functional genomics:

  • CRISPR Dependency Selection: We filter genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 essentiality screens across matching cell lines to isolate high-variance, high-skew gene knockout profiles that capture selective vulnerabilities.
  • Pairwise Correlation Vectors: For every compound in each screening dataset, we calculate pairwise-complete Pearson correlation coefficients between the compound's viability profile and gene knockout profiles across shared cell lines.
  • Functional Fingerprinting: This process maps each drug to a 2,755-dimensional functional effect vector. Because CRISPR KO is a causitive intervenetion, it avoids the simplest confounders typical of RNA-seq data.

Generative Manifold: Conditional VAE & Hierarchical SupCon

While functional fingerprints substantially enrich for biological signal, platform-specific technical batch effects persist in the raw correlation space. To construct a biologically structured representation space, MechMap implements a multi-objective Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE):

  • Encoder and Bottleneck: A 256-unit encoder with batch normalization, dropout, and leaky ReLU activations compresses the 2,755-dimensional fingerprints down to a 64-dimensional latent Gaussian distribution &mathcal;N(μ, σ²).
  • Source Conditioning: Screening platform identity (PRISM, CTD², GDSC1, GDSC2) is supplied as a one-hot conditional vector to the decoder. This forces the latent bottleneck to discard source-specific technical artifacts.
  • Hierarchical Supervised Contrastive Loss (SupCon): To organize the latent space by biological relevance, we introduce a tiered contrastive penalty that structures embeddings along a three-level biological hierarchy:
    • Same Compound across Screens (high weight): Pulls identical chemical entities together regardless of which platform measured them.
    • Shared Molecular Target (medium weight): Groups structurally distinct compounds that share a biological target (e.g. distinct PARP1/2 inhibitors).
    • Shared Biological Pathway (low weight): Clusters compounds acting within common regulatory circuits (e.g. DNA-damage response, MAPK signaling).

Automated Target & Pathway Curation with Gemini

Supervised contrastive learning requires comprehensive, clean, and standardized biological ground-truth labels. However, compound annotations across public databases (ChEMBL, PubChem, DrugBank, DepMap) are notoriously fragmented and sparse.

To solve this, I engineered an automated LLM curation pipeline using Google Gemini API:

  • Standardized Ontology: The pipeline annotated +4,000 compounds, resolving ambiguous chemical synonyms to canonical names, primary molecular targets (HGNC gene symbols), and hierarchical primary/secondary Reactome/GO pathways.
  • Confidence Scoring & Auditing: Each LLM-derived annotation is assigned a confidence metric and structured provenance metadata to reduce label noise prior to model training.

Biological Manifold Discovery

When projected via UMAP, raw pharmacological fingerprints segregate primarily into four isolated clusters corresponding to PRISM, CTD², GDSC1, and GDSC2. In contrast, the MechMap latent space's UMAP organizes compounds along biological mechanism. For example:

  • DNA-Damage Response (DDR): Co-localization of PARP inhibitors (olaparib, rucaparib, talazoparib), ATR/ATM kinase inhibitors, and topoisomerase poisons across disparate screening sources into a unified DDR territory.
  • Kinase Signaling Cascades: Distinct separation of receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR/ERBB, VEGFR) from downstream intracellular pathway modulators (MEK, RAF, PI3K/AKT/MTOR).

By projecting drugs of unknown mechanism into this latent manifold, MechMap allows biologists to infer the target from their nearest topological neighbors. It could also reveal off-target liabilities for known drugs.
